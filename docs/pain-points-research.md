# Pain Points in UK Construction Subcontractor Payment Workflows: A Practitioner-Grounded Feature Brief

## TL;DR
- The single most valuable problem a job-costing/subcontractor-payment SaaS can solve for small UK/Ireland builders is **notice-deadline compliance**: missing a Payment Notice or Pay Less Notice under the Construction Act (HGCRA 1996 as amended 2009) makes the subcontractor's applied-for sum the "notified sum," payable in full via a "smash-and-grab" adjudication — a purely administrative failure that Helix Law says occurs in "80% of cases," and which has cost real payers six-figure sums (e.g. £706,029.62 in Bexheat v Essex, lost by being one day late).
- The second-biggest buyer pain is the **manual, spreadsheet-and-Outlook grind of assessing subcontractor applications and running valuations** — vendor and practitioner sources converge on roughly 1–4 days of QS time per month per project on CVR/payment admin, with applications arriving late, in inconsistent formats, inflated, and without a breakdown that ties to the bill of quantities/activity schedule.
- On the subcontractor side, the loudest pain is **late payment, under-certification, and withheld retention** — with adjudication referrals at a record 2,264 in the year to April 2024 and "smash-and-grab"/technical payment claims the most common category (experienced by 63% of respondents). Existing tools (Payapps, Causeway, COINS, Procore) are widely used but practitioners report they are "too clunky for large/complex packages," force QSs back into Excel, and charge subcontractors per-claim fees that generate "kick-back."

## Key Findings

**1. Deadline/notice compliance is the highest-severity, most-defensible pain — and it is fundamentally an admin problem, not a legal one.** The Construction Act payment cycle is unforgiving: a Pay Less Notice served even one day late is invalid, and the full notified sum becomes payable regardless of the true value of the work. QS training body Metroun states plainly that "most smash and grab disputes happen because of poor administration rather than complex valuation disagreements" and that deadline dates "should not be left to memory." The King's College London / Adjudication Society third report, *Construction Adjudication in the United Kingdom: Tracing trends and guiding reform* (November 2024, authored by Prof Renato Nazzini and Aleksander Godhe), found the leading cause of adjudicated disputes — at 50% of respondents — was "inadequate contract administration," well ahead of "inappropriate contractor selection (causing only 1%)."

**2. Current deadline-tracking methods break down predictably.** Practitioners track notices in manual Excel sheets, Outlook, or memory. Mobarak Ali, a Graduate QS at Bouygues UK, described the failure mode exactly: applications were "tracked manually on an Excel sheet," and "the manual system also allowed the opportunity for payment notices or applications to be missed, if the application was sent directly to my email address... and I wasn't in work. A missed notice deadline could have huge financial implications for the company with payments then having to be made by law."

**3. The cost of a single miss is severe and well-documented.** In Bexheat Ltd v Essex Services Group, the payer missed the Pay Less deadline "by one day" and was ordered to pay the applied-for £706,029.62 plus VAT. In Bell Building Ltd v TClarke Contracting Ltd [2024] EWHC 1929 (TCC), an invalid Pay Less Notice in a smash-and-grab led the adjudicator to award the subcontractor £2,129,672.69 — more than it had originally sought. Helix Law reports that in "80% of cases the payer has not served a valid notice to pay less."

**4. Assessing subcontractor applications is a heavy, error-prone manual burden.** QS teams report spending 2–4 days per month compiling CVR packs (Planyard) and "two to three days each month just assembling the numbers." Applications routinely arrive late, in inconsistent formats (Excel, PDF, email attachments, handwritten amendments), inflated, and without a breakdown that maps to the BOQ/activity schedule, forcing the QS to reconstruct substantiation before they can certify.

**5. Existing tools have specific, publicly-voiced gaps.** The most telling review is a verified UK Capterra review of Payapps (now Autodesk-owned) by "Des" (Jan 2025, 2/5): the "BoQ/Activity schedule/Variation section is very clunky; full of interlocks (refusing entirely legitimate inputs)," and "if you're an 'advanced QS' you'll find yourself doing Excel assessments, fudging the same into PayApps and pasting the comment 'see attached spreadsheet' an awful lot." Cost is another gap: "Sofia" (3/5) notes "we get a lot of kick-back from subcontractors about the cost per claim."

**6. Retention and CIS are chronic, unmet admin pains.** BEIS's February 2020 consultation response estimated retentions withheld across UK construction at "£3.2bn to £5.9bn, with a central estimate of £4.5bn in 2015 prices" (a figure 71% of trade bodies agreed was reasonable). Late release is "the most frequent complaint," and tracking retention release dates across multiple projects "adds overhead that small businesses can ill [afford]." CIS adds a parallel compliance load — verify every subcontractor with HMRC before first payment (0%/20%/30%), keep timestamped evidence of the rate, re-verify after gaps, and file monthly returns.

## Details

### Regulatory context (why the pain exists)
UK construction payments run under the Housing Grants, Construction and Regeneration Act 1996 as amended by the Local Democracy, Economic Development and Construction Act 2009. Each subcontract runs a monthly cycle: the subcontractor submits an application; the payer must issue a Payment Notice (s.110A) stating the sum due; to pay less, the payer must serve a Pay Less Notice (s.111) before the final date for payment. Miss either, and the applied-for sum becomes the "notified sum" payable in full. "Pay-when-paid" clauses are unenforceable in UK law (except on upstream insolvency). The 2009 amendments tightened this regime — before it, main contractors could withhold with minimal explanation; now failure to serve a valid notice in time forfeits the right to pay less, full stop.

The consequence is a two-stage dispute architecture that practitioners must navigate: a "smash-and-grab" adjudication enforces the notified sum on the procedural point alone (ISG v Seevic; RMC v UK Construction, £248,053 enforced with no notice served), after which the payer can pursue a separate "true value" adjudication (Grove/S&T v Grove) — but only after paying the notified sum first (M Davenport v Greer). Bellway Homes v Surgo [2024] confirmed smash-and-grab and true-value can be argued in a single adjudication; VMA v Project One [2025] confirmed an unpaid notified sum is a defence to a true-value adjudication.

### Ranked pain points — MAIN CONTRACTOR / QS / COMMERCIAL MANAGER (the buyer)

**P1. Missing or defective Payment/Pay Less Notices (severity: critical; frequency: very high).** WHO: commercial managers, QSs, contract administrators. This is the highest-stakes pain and the clearest software wedge. It is an admin failure — Metroun: "poor administration rather than complex valuation disagreements." It recurs across every legal and practitioner source in the corpus. Triggers include programme slippage, staff being out of office, bank-holiday date miscalculations, wrong service method (the "Birmingham" example: emailed when only postal service was agreed → full amount paid), and serving early (Placefirst v CAR, 2025). UNMET: no lightweight tool for small builders auto-calculates the s.110A/s.111 deadlines per subcontract (accounting for the specific contract's mechanism and non-working days), surfaces them, and generates + timestamps proof of service.

**P2. Assessing/valuing applications (severity: high; frequency: monthly, every cycle).** WHO: QSs, AQSs. Applications arrive late, inconsistent, inflated, without breakdowns tying to the BOQ/activity schedule. The QS must verify work is actually done and the application is arithmetically correct before certifying. Time cost: 2–4 days/month on CVR packs (Planyard); Claire Hill (QS, Brown & Bancroft Interiors, Bolton) reported month-end CVRs "could usually take 3-4 days to put them together." UNMET: line-by-line assessment against the activity schedule with variation handling that doesn't force a fallback to Excel — the exact gap Payapps reviewers report.

**P3. Deadline tracking mechanics (severity: high; frequency: continuous).** WHO: QSs/commercial teams. Tools in use: Excel, Outlook, memory, ERP. Veritas Surveying's prescription — "Not Outlook. Not a scribble on the site wall. A proper, shared calendar everyone sees" — implicitly describes what teams do now and why it fails. A QS may run multiple projects/dozens of packages simultaneously, each with its own cycle dates.

**P4. Proving service / audit trail (severity: high in dispute; frequency: episodic but decisive).** WHO: commercial managers, contract administrators. "Prove you served it" moments decide smash-and-grab adjudications. UNMET for spreadsheet users: an immutable, timestamped record of what notice was served, when, by what method.

**P5. Retention tracking & release (severity: medium-high; frequency: ongoing).** WHO: QSs, finance. Two-tranche release (PC + end of defects), often across many projects; release dates "quietly forgotten." Tracking across multiple projects is explicit overhead for small firms.

**P6. CIS deduction & verification (severity: medium; frequency: every payment run + monthly returns).** WHO: contractor finance/QS. Verify with HMRC, apply 0/20/30%, retain evidence, re-verify after gaps, file CIS300. Get it wrong and "HMRC chases you for the shortfall — so you pay twice."

### Ranked pain points — SUBCONTRACTOR

**S1. Late payment / cash flow (severity: critical; frequency: endemic).** Per Build UK's own payment benchmarking data, "the average time taken to pay invoices by contractor members has reduced from 45 days to 29 days, and the percentage of invoices paid within 60 days has risen from 82% to 96%" since 2018 — but the wider picture is worse. DBT/London Economics research for the Small Business Commissioner (published 15 July 2025) found "Late payments are estimated to cost the UK economy almost £11 billion per year... 14,000 businesses close each year because of late payments, equivalent to 38 businesses every day," with over 1.5 million businesses (28%) affected and £26bn owed at any time. The raw emotion is captured in a November 2024 UK Business Forums thread where a subbie's partner describes being owed ~£25k: "it's sickening"; forum veterans reply that the industry "has a long and shameful history of this" and "'will be paid' is not paid... Paid is paid."

**S2. Under-certification / withheld sums without valid notices (severity: high; frequency: high).** WHO: subcontractors. The "we believe them to be too high for their margins" review dynamic — as one forum poster's contractor claimed, weeks after the invoice, with no Pay Less Notice served (which a moderator noted "would appear to be illegal").

**S3. Withheld retention (severity: high; frequency: very high).** The scale of loss is concrete: the 2018 BEIS research cited by LazyQS/The Access Group found "44% of contractors lost retentions entirely due to upstream insolvency, with an average loss of £79,900 per contractor" — the Carillion collapse being the canonical example. Main contractors also unlawfully link release to main-contract PC, contrary to the Act.

**S4. Application format/portal friction (severity: medium; frequency: every cycle).** WHO: subcontractors. Not knowing what backup to attach, which form/portal, and getting applications rejected or bounced to the next monthly cycle. Per-claim portal fees (Payapps) generate resentment.

**S5. "Pay-when-paid" culture and the smash-and-grab dynamic (severity: medium; frequency: cultural).** Subbies increasingly use smash-and-grab as their fastest lever; the threat itself often prompts settlement.

### The application-for-payment handoff (where the two sides meet)
This is the interface a SaaS product must own. What goes wrong: email attachments lost in inboxes (Ali's out-of-office miss); Excel/PDF/handwritten formats that don't tie to the schedule; missing line breakdowns and substantiation; overbilling that erodes trust; and unapproved variations included in the claim. Disputes escalate when substantiation is thin — the "client's QS knocks your application down because the substantiation is thin, and the argument eats a week" (Archdesk). Adjudication frequency is at a record high: 2,264 referrals in the year to April 2024 (+9% YoY, the highest on record; King's College London/Adjudication Society), with smash-and-grab/technical payment claims the single most common category ("experienced by 63%" of respondents) and most claims valued £125k–£500k. RICS remained the top nominating body, handling 1,340 of the 2,264 referrals.

### Workflow & admin burden (quantified)
- 2–4 days/month per QS team compiling CVR packs; "two to three days each month just assembling the numbers" (Planyard, vendor).
- Month-end CVRs "3-4 days" reduced to "10-15 minutes" with software (Claire Hill, QS, Brown & Bancroft — vendor testimonial).
- "A solid day, perhaps even a day and a half" per month sorting invoices for monthly payments (Paul Howarth, FD — vendor testimonial).
- Assessing/certifying applications cut "by around 50%" with dedicated software (Payapps customer quote — vendor).
- Most tedious/error-prone manual tasks named: reconciling application line items to the schedule, arithmetic checks, re-keying Excel assessments into portals, chasing substantiation, and manually diarising notice deadlines.

### Existing tools — gaps practitioners report
- **Payapps (Autodesk):** Strong for portal submission, notifications, single attachment location, sign-off circulation. Weak: "BoQ/Activity schedule/Variation section is very clunky; full of interlocks"; advanced QSs revert to Excel; per-claim cost draws subcontractor "kick-back"; "shame it only utilises payments and not more contract admin." Overall 4.5/5 on 37 Capterra reviews but Value for Money lowest at 4.1.
- **Causeway:** Trustpilot reviews (31) are mixed; users cite document-input difficulty and auto-renewing contracts with price rises. (Note: search results were partly polluted by unrelated "Causeway Coin" businesses; treat cautiously.)
- **COINS / Access Coins:** Deep construction ERP for tier-1s and top-75 homebuilders; overkill and slow to deploy for SMEs (per Site Samurai's competitive framing — vendor).
- **Site Samurai:** A UK-specific entrant explicitly built around s.111 Pay Less tracking, cumulative valuations, retention, CIS (0/20/30% with timestamped evidence), and a no-login client portal — indicating the market gap this brief identifies is already being targeted, but its CIS "verification" still requires manual HMRC portal use.
- **Procore/Viewpoint:** Broad platforms; UK Construction-Act-specific notice logic is not their core.

Feature requests voiced publicly: application portal with reminders + basic payment-notice front page + sign-off circulation + attachment space ("that would be plenty" — Des, Capterra); variations submittable before they're claimed (Dominic, Capterra); more contract admin beyond payments.

### Regulatory / legal friction practitioners mention
- Notice **validity content**: a Pay Less Notice must state the sum considered due and the basis of calculation; substance matters more than the label, but timing must still comply (Placefirst v CAR, 2025).
- **Timing/deadlines** relative to due date and final date for payment are the most common failure point.
- **2009 LDEDC amendments**: shifted power decisively to payees; ongoing confusion persists about compliant content and timing, and about whether early/combined notices are valid.
- **Retention reform / late-payment legislation**: the government's 2025 late-payments consultation (which ran 31 July–23 October 2025) and a proposed Commercial Payments Bill (which would ban retention clauses) signal a changing regulatory backdrop worth monitoring.

## Recommendations

**Stage 1 — Build the wedge: a Construction-Act notice engine (target: main contractor/QS buyer).** Ship first the feature no spreadsheet does safely: per-subcontract, auto-calculated due dates, final dates for payment, Payment Notice and Pay Less Notice deadlines — correctly handling each contract's payment mechanism and non-working/bank-holiday days — with escalating reminders, one-click compliant notice generation (stating sum due + basis), and timestamped proof-of-service capture. This directly attacks P1, P3, and P4, the highest-severity buyer pains. Benchmark to change course: if pilot QSs still keep a parallel Excel deadline tracker after 60 days, the reminder/visibility UX is not trusted — iterate before adding modules.

**Stage 2 — Own the application-for-payment handoff.** Give subcontractors a free, no-login (or low-friction, no per-claim-fee) submission portal that forces a line-by-line breakdown against the agreed activity schedule/BOQ and required backup, so the QS receives assessment-ready applications. Explicitly avoid the two Payapps complaints: (a) do not build "interlocks" that reject legitimate inputs — match Excel's flexibility; (b) do not charge subcontractors per claim. Benchmark: median QS assessment time per cycle should fall below one day; if QSs still export to Excel to assess, the line-item/variation UX has failed.

**Stage 3 — Automate retention and CIS.** Track retention withheld and both release tranches across all projects with automated release reminders; store CIS status (0/20/30%) per subcontractor with timestamped evidence and clean export to Xero/Sage. Pursue a genuine HMRC CIS verification integration as a differentiator, since even Site Samurai still requires manual portal verification.

**Stage 4 — Reporting layer.** Auto-generate CVRs from live valuation/payment data (the 2–4-day/month pain), giving directors a current commercial position rather than three-week-old numbers.

**Positioning.** Lead marketing with the smash-and-grab risk narrative to the buyer ("never lose a notice deadline again"; cite Bexheat's £706k, Helix's 80%), not with generic "streamline payments" language. Price on a flat, unlimited-user basis (as Site Samurai does) to avoid the per-seat/per-claim resentment that dogs incumbents, especially given small-firm budgets.

## Caveats
- **Source mix.** Many of the strongest quantified time-saving figures (Planyard, Payapps testimonials, ProcurePro anecdotes, Site Samurai claims) originate from software vendors or their case studies. They are directionally consistent (~1–4 days/month per QS on CVR/payment admin) but should be read as vendor-sourced, not independent research. Independent/practitioner evidence (Capterra reviews, the UK Business Forums thread, King's College adjudication data, BEIS/DBT/GOV.UK statistics, Build UK benchmarking, and case law) is flagged as such throughout and carries more weight.
- **Forum voice thinner than ideal.** Genuine raw Reddit/r/QuantitySurveying/TalkConstruction threads with attributable QS quotes did not surface well in search; the richest first-person practitioner material came from a UK Business Forums thread, one named Payapps case study (Mobarak Ali), and Capterra reviews. A manual sweep of Reddit and LinkedIn would strengthen the qualitative base.
- **Helix Law's "80%"** is a firm's own caseload observation, not a formal survey; treat as indicative.
- **Some anecdotes are illustrative.** The ProcurePro £400k and "Birmingham/Leeds" examples are written by an ex-QS author and partly concern EOT/LD notices rather than pay-less notices specifically; Bexheat v Essex and Bell v TClarke are the fully verifiable cases with exact figures.
- **US contamination.** Several search results (AIA G702/G703, lien waivers, "50-day" payment data from Built/Billd) describe US practice and were excluded from UK-specific findings.
- **Fast-moving regulation.** Late-payment reform, the Fair Payment Code, and a proposed retention ban were in flux as of 2025–2026; verify current status before publishing marketing claims tied to them.
