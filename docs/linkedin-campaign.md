## 0. The core idea (read this first)

You are not "marketing a SaaS." You are a **QS who got tired of watching the industry lose money to missed paperwork**, talking to other QS/commercial people about a problem they already lose sleep over. That's the entire campaign. Every post, comment and DM runs on three rules:

1. **You, the founder, are the channel** — not a company page. People follow people. Post from your personal profile.
2. **Pain first, product second.** 80% of content is about the problem (smash-and-grab claims, missed Pay Less deadlines, spreadsheet chaos). 20% mentions NoticeGuard at all.
3. **Conversations are the goal, not likes.** Every comment thread and DM is a potential pilot. The waitlist (landing page) is just where they park.

**The funnel:** Post → they comment/react → you reply and connect → 2–3 touches later, soft DM → pilot conversation → waitlist link if not ready.

## 3. Content pillars (the 5 post types you rotate)

| # | Pillar | Purpose | Share of posts |
|---|--------|---------|----------------|
| 1 | **Pain stories** — smash-and-grab mechanics, deadline horror stories | Makes them feel seen | 30% |
| 2 | **Educational** — HGCRA notice rules explained simply, worked examples | Builds authority | 25% |
| 3 | **Build in public** — what you're building, why, screenshots, lessons | Makes them root for you | 20% |
| 4 | **Opinion / hot takes** — "spreadsheets aren't compliance systems" | Drives comments/shares | 15% |
| 5 | **Polls & questions** — "have you ever missed a deadline?" | Cheap reach + market research | 10% |

**Cadence:** 3 posts/week (Mon / Wed / Fri), posted **7:30–8:30am UK** (site-start scroll time). Consistency beats brilliance — 3 good posts a week for 12 weeks beats 10 in week one and silence after.

**Format rules:**
- Text-first. First line = the hook; it decides whether "…see more" gets clicked.
- Short paragraphs. One idea per line. White space is your friend.
- **No external links in the post body** (LinkedIn suppresses them). Put the landing page link in the first comment.
- End with a genuine question to start the comment thread.
- Reply to **every** comment within a few hours — replies double the reach.

> Payment Notice vs Pay Less Notice — the difference can cost you six figures:
>
> → Payment Notice: what you say you'll pay, served shortly after the due date.

>
> Miss the Payment Notice? The subcontractor can serve their own, and it becomes the default.
> Miss the Pay Less? You pay the full applied amount. No argument about value.
>
> Same contract, different deadlines, different consequences. Both tracked per cycle, per subcontract.
>
> This is the bit spreadsheets get wrong. Not the rules — the arithmetic of dates.

## 5. The daily engagement routine (20 min/day — this is where pilots actually come from)

Posting is 30% of the campaign. This is the other 70%:

- **10 min:** comment genuinely on 3–5 posts from target buyers and construction-law voices. Real comments (add an angle, a story, a question) — not "Great post!" Their audience sees you; the author remembers you.
- **5 min:** send the day's 10–15 connection requests (Section 2).
- **5 min:** reply to every comment on your own posts. Every reply = more reach + a conversation started.

**DM ladder** (when someone engages with your content 2–3 times, or accepts a connection and fits the profile):
1. **Touch 1 (question, not pitch):** "Thanks for engaging with the deadline posts — are you on the commercial side? Curious how your team tracks notice dates today."
2. **Touch 2 (only if they reply):** share one relevant war story + ask about theirs.
3. **Touch 3 (only if warm):** "We're onboarding a few pilot contractors — no cost, I set up your first contract with you. Worth 20 minutes?"
Never skip a rung. Never pitch in the first message.

## 7. Realistic expectations

- **Weeks 1–2:** feels like shouting into the void. Normal. Keep posting.
- **Weeks 3–6:** repeat commenters appear; DMs start; first pilot conversation.
- **Weeks 6–12:** 20–50 waitlist names, 3–5 genuine pilot conversations — IF the cadence and daily engagement hold.
- The #1 failure mode is quitting at week 3. The #2 failure mode is posting product updates instead of pain. Avoid both.
