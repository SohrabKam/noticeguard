# AI in UK Construction Payment & Commercial Management Software: A Founder's Evidence Review (2023–2026)

## TL;DR
- **Document extraction is production-ready for clean, structured pay applications but degrades sharply on the handwritten and non-standard documents that dominate real subcontractor submissions** — plan for 95–99% field accuracy on machine-printed AIA/valuation PDFs but ~70% effective accuracy on the handwritten mix, and make human-in-the-loop review with confidence scoring and reconciliation mandatory before any figure drives a Payment Notice.
- **Genuine AI for *assessing* payment applications (line-by-line valuation, anomaly/duplicate detection, progress verification) is still nascent in the UK** — most incumbent "AI" claims (Payapps, Causeway, BuildQS) are really automation/standardisation, while the most defensible technical building blocks (OpenSpace/Disperse and Buildots for verified percent-complete, Causeway for AP fraud/anomaly flagging, contract-review LLMs for payment-clause extraction) are real but sit adjacent to your core workflow. This is a genuine product opportunity.
- **The regulatory clock is the single most important design constraint**: RICS's mandatory *Responsible use of AI in surveying practice* standard took effect **9 March 2026**, requiring a named accountable surveyor, written risk registers, an AI systems register, dip-sampling of automated outputs, and advance written client notice — build audit logging, confidence thresholds and explainability in from day one, not as an afterthought.

## Key Findings

1. **Extraction accuracy is bimodal.** Vendor and benchmark evidence converges on 95–99% field-level accuracy for clean machine-printed invoices/pay-apps (Azure Document Intelligence, AWS Textract, Google Document AI, GPT-4o+OCR), but independent construction-specific testing shows most tools fall to ~70% effective accuracy on the handwritten/hand-annotated documents that make up the majority of real subcontractor submissions.
2. **Straight-through-processing (STP) of 85–95% is realistic only after tuning on a firm's own document mix**, and only when low-confidence fields are routed to human review. Merged cells, multi-page valuations, handwritten margin amendments and inconsistent per-subcontractor layouts remain the dominant failure modes.
3. **Assessment AI in the UK payment space is largely aspirational.** Payapps (acquired by Autodesk for ~$390m in February 2024 — not Eque2/Rimini) and Eque2 (an integration partner, not the owner) provide standardisation and "self-assess" workflows, not AI valuation. Causeway's CausewayOne is marketed as "AI-first" and does apply AI to AP fraud/anomaly flagging. C-Link's AI is real but targets pre-award tender analysis, not interim valuation.
4. **The strongest independent building block for anomaly detection on claimed progress is visual progress verification** — OpenSpace (which acquired Disperse) and Buildots generate verified percent-complete per trade/zone that can be cross-checked against claimed percentages, though both keep humans in the loop and neither publishes rigorous accuracy benchmarks.
5. **Forecasting has the deepest academic evidence base but the thinnest construction-payment-specific validation.** ML models (random forests, gradient boosting, ANNs, Bayesian networks) consistently beat regression/S-curve baselines in cost/final-account prediction papers, and treasury-sector benchmarks show large error reductions, but there is no strong independent study quantifying AI cash-out forecasting from UK payment-cycle data specifically.
6. **Compliance/contract-review LLMs (Luminance, Robin AI, Kira, Spellbook, Document Crunch) can extract payment terms and flag onerous clauses**, which maps directly onto Construction Act deadline setup — but all vendors and independent commentators insist AI is a first-pass reviewer, not a substitute for legal judgement.
7. **Adoption is low and ROI evidence is almost entirely vendor-supplied.** RICS's 2025 AI-in-Construction survey found 45% of organisations report no AI use and just 1% have scaled it across projects (34% in early pilots); every quantified payment-workflow ROI figure found is a vendor testimonial, not an independent study.
8. **Risk and liability are now formal regulatory matters.** The RICS AI standard (effective 9 March 2026) makes human accountability, audit trails and client transparency mandatory, and PI insurers/law firms are actively warning that traditional policies may not cover AI-related errors.

## Details

### 1. Document AI / Extraction

**State of the art.** The mainstream document-AI platforms are all viable back-ends: Azure AI Document Intelligence's prebuilt invoice model, AWS Textract, Google Document AI, plus LLM-based extraction (GPT-4o, Claude, Gemini) and specialist IDP platforms (Rossum, ABBYY Vantage, Hypatos, Nanonets, Docsumo). A 2025 head-to-head invoice benchmark (Businesswaretech) found Azure Document Intelligence and GPT-4o+third-party OCR led on field accuracy, with a maximum out-of-the-box extraction quality of about 97% (GPT-4o+OCR); Google Document AI's invoice parser performed worst in most tests; all services cost roughly $10 per 1,000 invoices, but GPT-4o approaches were far slower (16–33 seconds/page vs 2–4 seconds for AWS/Azure/Google). Microsoft documents that Document Intelligence outputs structured JSON with per-field confidence scores, enabling automatic routing of high-confidence extractions and human review of low-confidence ones.

**Realistic accuracy for 2025–2026.** Vendor/benchmark claims of "95–99% accuracy" (Extend, Azure, MightyBot's "99%+" for Built Technologies construction-loan draws) are consistently qualified as applying to clean, machine-printed documents. The most useful independent-style evidence is a 2026 construction-specific test (ImageToTable.ai) of eight tools on 35 real construction documents — AIA G702/G703 pay applications, subcontractor invoices (~70% handwritten/hand-annotated), timesheets and delivery notes — which found that on a realistic mix where ~70% of documents arrive handwritten, most tools land at roughly 70% effective accuracy ("not automation, just a data entry desk you pay for"), with the best tool scoring 94% on printed and 88% on handwritten content. Enterprise STP rates of 85–95% are cited (Signisys) but only after optimisation and with human review of unusual layouts, poor scans and handwriting.

**Failure modes.** Documented failure modes directly relevant to valuations: handwritten margin amendments and additional line items; inconsistent per-subcontractor layouts (defeating template-based parsers); merged cells and borderless tables; multi-page G703-style continuation sheets; and silent model drift — a real Azure support thread from April 2025 shows the prebuilt invoice model suddenly ceasing to return specific date fields after an internal model update, a serious reliability risk for a payment-critical pipeline.

**QA/validation before figures are used.** Best-practice pattern, consistent across sources: per-field confidence scoring with low-confidence routing to review queues; mandatory human-in-the-loop checkpoints for consequential figures; automated post-processing that flags any numeric claim not traceable to a verified source; and reconciliation checks (e.g., cross-document reconciliation across draw packages, three-way matching of application vs schedule of values vs prior certifications). For your product, the reconciliation opportunity is strong: extracted line totals should be validated against the activity schedule/BoQ and previous certified cycles before any figure enters a Payment Notice.

### 2. Assessment Assistance

**Reality check.** Dedicated research confirms that genuine AI applied specifically to *assessing subcontractor interim payment applications* is nascent in the UK; most incumbent "AI" claims are standardisation/automation. Key named players:

- **Payapps** (UK/Ireland/ANZ/US; Autodesk agreed to acquire it on 24 January 2024 and completed for ~$390m on 20 February 2024; US brand GCPay). Autodesk's Jim Lynch noted at acquisition that "in construction, the payments process is often a manual process." Payapps processes very large volumes of applications for payment globally. Its "self-assess" feature is a workflow tool — "you set up the contract in the system and your suppliers then provide you with consistent information" — not AI valuation. Marketing claims it "minimised the human error impact." It acquired UK firm WebContractor in 2023 to deepen Construction Act-compliant subcontractor workflows. **Assessment: automation/standardisation, not AI assessment.**
- **Causeway** (UK). CausewayOne is marketed as an "AI-first," "construction-native" platform. Its most concrete AI claim is in AP: "AI checks every invoice against known risk patterns before payment is released," flagging changed supplier bank details, duplicate invoice numbers/amounts, tax-treatment mismatches, and payments about to breach a Fair Payment Code commitment. This is genuine anomaly/fraud detection but oriented to AP invoice processing, not line-by-line subcontractor valuation. Vendor marketing; no independent verification found.
- **C-Link** (UK, built by QSs). Real "QSAI" AI feature, but for **pre-award tender/bid analysis and procurement**, not interim valuation. Customer quote: the AI "saves a lot of time for the QSs; instead of manually inputting prices, it's done 90% for you." Vendor-supplied.
- **COINS, Union Square** (UK): established commercial-management/ERP incumbents whose job descriptions dominate QS recruitment; not marketed as AI valuation assistants.
- **US pay-app validation AI** (Kolena, Mirage Metrics, Rabbet, MightyBot): describe AI that ingests the schedule of values, cross-references daily progress reports and site photos, detects duplicate/inflated billing, and flags line items exceeding contractual unit prices. These are US-focused marketing sources, not independently verified, but they describe exactly the assessment feature set your product targets.

**The strongest independent building block — progress verification.** For anomaly detection on claimed percentages vs actual progress, computer-vision progress tracking is the most mature adjacent technology:
- **OpenSpace** (acquired **Disperse** in 2025) pairs 360° capture with AI classifiers plus expert human verification to produce verified percent-complete per trade/zone, explicitly to "validate work-in-place for billing" and "resolve disputes and issue payments faster." It integrates with P6, Asta Powerproject, MS Project and Excel. A customer (Commodore Construction) reports spotting productivity issues "as early as 10% completion."
- **Buildots** compares 360° helmet-camera imagery against BIM to calculate per-trade, per-zone completion; described by third parties as high-accuracy on complex commercial projects but premium-priced and BIM-dependent.
- Neither publishes rigorous, independent field-level accuracy benchmarks; both deliberately keep humans in the loop, which is itself the defensible design pattern.

**Academic/technique base.** Anomaly detection on payment line items maps onto well-established statistical methods (e.g., per-payee, per-component distribution models flagging deviations beyond N standard deviations, as in payroll-anomaly patents) and financial fraud-detection ML. This is a solved technique class; the construction-specific novelty is comparing claimed percentages against activity-schedule rates, prior cycles and progress evidence.

### 3. Forecasting

**Academic evidence is deep and consistent.** Multiple 2020–2025 studies show ML beating traditional regression/S-curve methods for construction cost and final-account prediction: decision trees, random forests, gradient boosting and multiple linear regression benchmarked on New York City capital projects (≥$25m); hybrid Structural Equation Modeling + Bayesian Networks for final time/cost; ANNs on Scottish water projects; and explainable-AI/ensemble comparative studies. The dominant metric is reduction in MAPE (mean absolute percentage error) vs baselines.

**Cross-sector treasury benchmarks quantify the gains** — but not for construction payments specifically. theStacc, citing the 2025 AFP Treasury Benchmarking Survey (600+ treasury teams across North America and Europe), reports manual cash forecasts averaged 78% accuracy at the 4-week horizon and dropped to 60% at 13 weeks, versus 94% at 4 weeks and 88–92% at 13 weeks for AI treasury agents. The same source cites Visa 2025 research showing traditional working-capital forecasts ran a 68% variance against actuals (a machine-learning improvement figure was cited but is not corroborated in the located primary source, so treat the 68% baseline as the reliable datapoint). J.P. Morgan states AI can reduce forecasting error rates by up to 50% vs traditional methods. **Caveat: these are treasury/corporate figures, not construction pay-cycle figures, and several are vendor/bank-published.**

**The gap:** no strong independent study was found quantifying AI cash-out forecasting from UK payment-cycle data (valuation drift, retention release timing, certification lag, final-account outcomes) — which is precisely your differentiator. The S-curve remains the incumbent method; the opportunity is to learn per-subcontractor and per-employer certification-lag and valuation-drift patterns from your own assessed-but-unpaid cycle data.

### 4. Compliance & Risk (Construction Act deadlines & contract terms)

**The statutory spine.** Under the HGCRA 1996 (as amended by the LDEDC Act 2009), the payer must issue a Payment Notice (s.110A) and, to pay less than the notified sum, a valid Pay Less Notice (s.111) before the final date for payment; missing these deadlines makes the payee's application the notified sum, enabling a "smash and grab" adjudication for the full amount regardless of true value (ISG v Seevic; qualified by Grove v S&T, which restored the payer's right to a subsequent "true value" adjudication after paying). Adjudicators enforce strict compliance with contractual/statutory deadlines and notice validity — which is exactly what a deadline-tracking engine must encode.

**LLM contract-review tools that map onto this.** For automatically extracting payment terms, notice periods and onerous clauses from subcontracts/JCT/NEC at contract setup:
- **Luminance** ("Legal-Grade AI," Cambridge-founded, "Panel of Judges" multi-model architecture) — "Ask Lumi" extracts parties, contract value and payment terms across document sets and flags non-standard clauses in Word.
- **Robin AI** — clause-level review with configurable risk scores, playbook-based redlines, obligation tracking for deadlines/renewals, exportable audit logs, "human in the loop by design."
- **Kira** (Litera), **Spellbook** (in-Word, flags non-standard payment terms like "pay-when-paid" deviating from Net 30), **Document Crunch** (construction-specific contract intelligence), and construction-trained **Lexilio** (FIDIC/NEC/JCT).
- General-purpose LLMs (Claude/GPT) are already used by QSs (per BuildCopilot, C-Link) to summarise contract clauses and prepare valuations, but guides explicitly warn they should not give contractual/legal advice or produce final prices without checking.

**Design implication:** an LLM can reliably *extract* the payment mechanism (due dates, notice windows, retention %, CIS status) into structured fields to drive deadline calculations, but the calculation/alerting logic should be deterministic, not LLM-generated, given hallucination risk on dates/numbers.

### 5. Adoption & ROI Evidence

**Adoption (independent, industry-body sources).**
- RICS *AI in Construction 2025* (2,200+ professionals globally): "45% of organisations report no AI use, with just 1% having scaled AI across projects"; Construction Dive's read of the same data notes 34% in early pilot phases and only ~1.5% reporting AI use across multiple processes; ~70% of PMs/QSs believe AI will help deliver greater value, but 38–44% report concern about AI's impact on their role. Barriers: skills shortage (46%), integration/siloed systems (37%), poor data quality (30%).
- RICS *Digitalisation in Construction 2024*: "just 32% of quantity surveyors... are using digital tools on most or all of their projects across six key processes, a modest two percentage point increase year-on-year," with progress monitoring/H&S highest at 41% and cost estimation second at 38%.
- A 2023 RICS survey (cited by Helium42) found only 12% of QSs possess competence in AI/ML concepts.
- CIOB published a free *Artificial Intelligence Playbook* (2024), positioning AI as a "new colleague" for repetitive data tasks, aimed especially at SMEs.

**ROI (all vendor-supplied — treat with caution).**
- Payapps testimonial: "saves us three or four days' administration time a month."
- BW: Workplace Experts (Theo Riakiotakis): "we're saving around £100k a year now" (note: Payapps' own site inconsistently attributes a near-identical quote to a Bouygues UK director on another page).
- C-Link: "up to 47% of a QS's time in procurement"; "600 hours of QS time per project" (also stated as 400 hours elsewhere — internal inconsistency).
- US pay-app AI marketing (Mirage Metrics): claims of catching "98% of overbilling errors" and cutting payment cycles from 60 to 35 days — illustrative marketing, not verified.

**The honest position for your investors/customers:** there is **no robust independent study quantifying time saved or error reduction for AI-assisted interim payment-application assessment in the UK.** This is both a credibility risk (don't overclaim) and a wedge (be the first to publish rigorous, independently measurable metrics from your own deployments).

### 6. Risks & Limits

**Hallucinated figures are the defining risk for a payment product.** Finance/audit-sector evidence (Trullion, EY, the arXiv "FAITH" framework on tabular financial hallucinations) shows LLMs can produce plausible but fabricated numbers that "look like accurate outputs," with hallucination risk scaling with document complexity and volume — exactly the conditions of a multi-page valuation. In a payment context, a hallucinated line total flowing into a Payment Notice or Pay Less Notice could directly cause an over- or under-certification and, if a Pay Less Notice is thereby invalid or late, expose the contractor to a smash-and-grab for the full applied sum.

**Liability and auditability.** The Air Canada tribunal precedent (organisations are liable for their AI's outputs) is widely cited. RICS's standard now makes a **named, qualified surveyor** responsible for AI outputs with a "material impact," and requires the reliability decision to be recorded in writing.

**RICS *Responsible use of AI in surveying practice*** (1st edition published 10 September 2025; the PDF states it is "effective from 9 March 2026"; CMS confirms it "imposes mandatory requirements on RICS members and regulated firms" globally where AI has a "material impact"). Mandatory duties include: recording the material-impact determination in writing; baseline AI knowledge; a written assessment that AI is the most appropriate tool; a **written register of AI systems** (purpose, first-use date, next review date); **risk registers**; supplier due diligence; **professional judgement with a named accountable surveyor** signing off reliability; **randomised dip-sampling** of automated/high-volume outputs; and **advance written client notice** of when/how AI is used, the extent of PI cover for AI use, redress processes, and how to opt out. RICS frames this as putting "the surveyor's judgement, rather than the AI system's output, at the heart of professional practice," and the standard will be a benchmark in negligence proceedings.

**Professional indemnity insurance.** Law firms and brokers (CMS, Beale & Co, Mills & Reeve, Lockton, WTW, Browne Jacobson) warn that traditional PI policies may not cover AI-related errors; that terms of engagement must now disclose AI use and the extent of PI cover; that there is as yet no reported case law on AI in a PI context; and that some policies already contain exclusions for "automated decision-making without human oversight." There is no CIOB-specific PI/liability standard for AI valuation; RICS is the governing instrument.

**Mitigations practitioners are adopting** (and that your product should productise): human-in-the-loop review for all consequential figures; per-field confidence thresholds with low-confidence routing; provenance/audit logging tracing every extracted figure to its source document location; deterministic (non-LLM) calculation and deadline logic; reconciliation against activity schedules and prior cycles; dip-sampling of automated outputs; and explainability (showing *why* a line was flagged).

## Recommendations

**Stage 1 — Build the trustworthy extraction+reconciliation core (0–6 months).**
- Use a proven document-AI back-end (Azure Document Intelligence or GPT-4o+OCR led the 2025 benchmarks) but do **not** rely on out-of-the-box accuracy. Fine-tune/custom-train on your customers' actual submission mix.
- Treat every extracted figure as provisional: per-field confidence scores, mandatory human review below a tuned threshold, and automatic reconciliation of extracted line items against the activity schedule/BoQ and previous certified cycles. **Benchmark to change course:** if effective field accuracy on your real (handwritten-inclusive) document mix is below ~90% after tuning, keep extraction assistive-only and do not auto-populate assessed figures.
- Log provenance for every figure (source doc, page, coordinates) from day one — this is both an audit and an RICS-compliance asset.

**Stage 2 — Layer assessment assistance as flags, not decisions (6–12 months).**
- Start with deterministic/statistical anomaly detection (line deviates from prior cycle, exceeds contract rate, duplicate claim, claimed % implausible vs prior %) — this is low-hallucination-risk and explainable. Reserve LLMs for narrative/explanation, not for producing the numbers.
- Integrate progress evidence: partner with or emulate OpenSpace/Buildots-style verified percent-complete to flag claimed-vs-verified gaps. This is your clearest defensible differentiator against smash-and-grab exposure caused by rushed assessments.

**Stage 3 — Deadline-compliance engine + forecasting (9–18 months).**
- Use an LLM to *extract* payment mechanisms (due dates, notice windows, retention %, CIS status) from subcontracts/JCT/NEC into structured fields, but drive Payment Notice / Pay Less Notice deadline alerts with deterministic logic. Flag onerous/non-standard clauses at contract setup (the Luminance/Robin AI/Document Crunch pattern).
- Build cash-out forecasting from your own assessed-but-unpaid cycle data, learning per-employer certification lag and per-subcontractor valuation drift. Baseline against the S-curve and **publish your MAPE improvement** — this is the independent evidence the market currently lacks.

**Stage 4 — Compliance-by-design and evidence (continuous).**
- Ship RICS-standard features as product: an AI systems register, risk register templates, dip-sampling tooling, named-approver sign-off on AI-influenced assessments, and client-facing AI-use disclosure. Market compliance as a feature — your buyers (£10m–£250m contractors employing MRICS QSs) are directly bound by the 9 March 2026 standard.
- Commission or run an independent, measurable pilot (time-per-valuation, error rate, adjudication-exposure reduction) and publish it. **Benchmark to change strategy:** if independent measurement shows no material time saving vs standardisation-only tools, reposition around risk reduction (missed-deadline prevention) rather than efficiency.

## Caveats
- **Vendor vs independent evidence:** Almost all accuracy and ROI figures in the payment/QS space are vendor-supplied marketing (Payapps testimonials, C-Link, Causeway, Mirage Metrics, MightyBot). The genuinely independent evidence is thinner: the RICS/CIOB surveys (adoption), the ImageToTable construction extraction test (handwriting accuracy, though itself a vendor blog with a commercial interest), and the academic forecasting/hallucination literature. Categories are flagged throughout.
- **Ownership/attribution corrections:** Payapps was acquired by Autodesk (~$390m, completed February 2024), not Eque2 or Rimini — Eque2 is only an integration partner. The £100k/year saving is most reliably attributed to BW: Workplace Experts, with an attribution inconsistency on Payapps' own site.
- **Unverifiable vendors:** "Trustia" and "Sync-Simple" could not be verified as real AI payment/valuation vendors; "Aphex" is a scheduling tool and "Flowlens" is manufacturing MRP — likely mis-categorised for this use case. Treat these names with caution.
- **Construction-payment-specific forecasting evidence is thin:** the strong ML-beats-baseline results are for cost/final-account prediction or cross-sector treasury forecasting, not UK interim-payment cash-out forecasting specifically.
- **Regulatory dates:** The RICS standard's clause-level numbering was summarised from law-firm briefings (CMS, Beale & Co) rather than read clause-by-clause from the primary PDF; the substance (mandatory obligations, 9 March 2026 effective date) is well-corroborated, including directly from the RICS PDF's own effective-date statement.
- **Model drift is a live operational risk:** documented cases show cloud document-AI models silently changing extracted fields after provider updates — a payment pipeline needs regression testing against a golden document set.
